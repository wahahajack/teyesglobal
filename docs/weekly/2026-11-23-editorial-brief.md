# Frozen editorial brief

## Candidate decision

Approved for drafting, subject to independent review:

**EU CRA for Android Head Unit Importers: A Supplier-Diligence Checklist After EN 40000-1-2**

## Reader and job to be done

- Primary reader: EU importer, distributor, wholesale buyer or compliance/product manager evaluating connected aftermarket Android head units.
- Search intent: understand whether the automotive exclusion can be assumed, what changed in November 2026, and what evidence to request from a supplier before the main CRA obligations apply.
- Concrete problem: turn general CRA material into a product- and SKU-level supplier due-diligence workflow without making a legal conclusion for every head unit.

## Mandatory scope and caveats

- Explain the Commission's 17 November automotive-component test accurately.
- Explain what EN 40000-1-1 and EN 40000-1-2 do and do not establish.
- Separate current reporting obligations from the 11 December 2027 main-application date.
- Do not state that all aftermarket head units are in scope.
- Do not state that EN 40000-1-2 is a product certificate or, without verification, that conformance automatically gives presumption of conformity.
- Do not claim TEYES, CC4 PRO or any SKU is CRA-compliant or certified.
- Treat the article as procurement guidance, not legal advice.

## Incremental value

1. A scope-assessment decision sequence tailored to connected aftermarket head units.
2. A procurement checklist covering exact hardware/software identity, support period, vulnerability intake, update process, documentation and importer records.
3. A list of claims importers should not accept or repeat without evidence.
4. Practical treatment of mixed-model orders: group variants only where security-relevant architecture and risk are genuinely shared.
