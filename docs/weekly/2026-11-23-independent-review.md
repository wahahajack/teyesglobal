### Factual and source-fidelity detail

1. **The automotive scope explanation is substantially faithful.** The OJ guidance says components are outside the CRA where they are exclusively designed and constructed for integration into vehicles covered by Regulations (EU) 2019/2144 and No 168/2013, clearly intended and suitable only for that integration. It contrasts those with generic components and says public retail or online channels are relevant objective conditions; restricted automotive B2B channels may indicate exclusivity. The draft preserves the essential “may indicate, not automatic” distinction.

2. **The guidance chronology needs correction.** The Commission announced its guidance on 27 July 2026. The guidance then appeared in OJ C/2026/6013 dated 17 November 2026. Saying simply, “On 17 November 2026, the European Commission published guidance,” implies first publication in November and makes the section's novelty claim less precise than the primary record permits. The accurate formulation is that the guidance was issued in July and published in the Official Journal on 17 November, or that the 17 November OJ publication made the existing guidance formally available there.

3. **The standards account is materially accurate.** The Commission page dated 19 November says the first supporting standard was published on 18 November; CEN-CENELEC's 19 November announcement identifies both EN 40000-1-1 and EN 40000-1-2. The draft accurately distinguishes vocabulary from lifecycle/risk-management principles and says more detailed standards remain in development.

4. **The standards' legal status needs one more sentence.** Article 27 gives a presumption of conformity only for the requirements covered by harmonised standards whose references have been published in the OJEU. I found no official OJEU citation for EN 40000-1-1 or EN 40000-1-2 as of 23 November 2026. The draft correctly says EN 40000-1-2 is not a product certificate and does not prove full conformity, but it should explicitly state that availability of the standard is not the same as OJEU citation and does not itself create an Article 27 presumption of conformity.

5. **The application dates are correct.** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 applies generally from 11 December 2027, while Article 14 reporting duties apply from 11 September 2026. The draft correctly assigns Article 14 notifications to manufacturers rather than importers.

6. **The support-period explanation omits a central rule.** Article 13(8) requires a support period of at least five years unless the product is expected to be in use for less than five years, in which case the period corresponds to expected use. Products expected to remain in use longer should receive longer support. Because “CRA support period” is a target phrase and the section is commercial guidance, omitting the minimum is a substantive usefulness and accuracy gap.

7. **The importer framing is incomplete unless explicitly narrowed.** Article 19 also covers, among other matters, pre-market verification, CE marking and the EU declaration, manufacturer and importer identification, non-conformity handling, informing the manufacturer of vulnerabilities, regulatory notifications for significant risk, and retaining the EU declaration for at least 10 years after placement on the market or for the support period, whichever is longer. The draft's table is valuable supplier due diligence, but it is not a full Article 19 checklist. The line saying an importer does not need every confidential engineering file “to begin diligence” is defensible only if immediately qualified: the questionnaire does not displace the documents an importer must be able to provide or make available under Article 19.

### Buyer usefulness and originality

The strongest section is the table. Its third column converts abstract obligations into recognizable supplier red flags without pretending a weak answer automatically proves non-compliance. The product-family treatment is also useful: screen size and cosmetic variation are distinguished from connectivity modules, software stacks and update mechanisms. That follows the Commission guidance closely and gives wholesale buyers a practical mixed-order workflow.

The article's original contribution is therefore real: it applies official horizontal guidance to a specific aftermarket procurement situation. It does not manufacture a compliance outcome for TEYES or any model. The CTA is proportionate and expressly requires confirmation for the exact market and configuration.

### Blind wording and legal-risk notes

- Retain “general commercial guidance, not legal advice,” but do not rely on that disclaimer to cure an over-broad checklist title.
- Replace unqualified statements that a component “falls within the CRA” with language tied to the Commission guidance: “the Commission guidance treats that component as within scope,” followed by the product-specific assessment caveat.
- Name Regulations (EU) 2019/2144 and No 168/2013 in the body, not only the source record.
- Preserve the statement that restricted B2B automotive distribution is an indicator rather than a safe harbour.
- Preserve the express denial of any TEYES/CC4 PRO scope, conformity or certification claim.
- Do not add “harmonised,” “certified,” “approved,” or “CRA ready” to descriptions of EN 40000-1-2 unless the exact legal basis and current OJEU status are rechecked at publication.
